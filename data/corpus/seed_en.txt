Mina keeps a notebook on a square table in a small workshop. The first line she writes is her own: a model can only repeat patterns it has actually seen.
In the morning she slices bread, pours tea, and opens the window. Cold air moves the page. She writes that the kettle clicks when the water boils.
Her brother Adel repairs a loose chair. He counts the screws out loud: one, two, three, four. Mina copies the count into the notebook because she heard it in the room.
They walk to the garden after lunch. The garden has beans, mint, and a crooked fig tree. Adel ties the fig branch with cloth. Mina writes: the cloth is blue, the branch is thin, the soil is damp.
A neighbor asks where the text came from. Mina says it came from this yard and this table, not from a book she does not own. She will not paste someone else's story into the file.
At night the lamp is dim. The cat sleeps beside the chair. Rain ticks on the roof. Mina adds one more line: training on a short note can memorize the note, and that is not the same as knowing the world.
The next day they describe the market. Red apples. A heavy bag. A bus that arrives late. Adel says the bus door sticks, and Mina writes the sentence as he said it.
This file is a seed for a local experiment. It is short on purpose. Add much more text you have the right to use before expecting the model to sound fluent.
